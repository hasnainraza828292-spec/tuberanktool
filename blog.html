How YouTube Tags Really Work

Tags are keywords you add privately in YouTube Studio. Viewers don't normally see them, and YouTube has said they play only a small part in helping people find videos.

Where they can help: if your topic is often misspelled or has several common names, a tag can cover those variations. Where they don't help: adding dozens of loosely related terms. That doesn't make a video more discoverable, and irrelevant tags can break YouTube's policies.

A practical approach is to add one tag for your main topic, a few close variations and a couple of specific phrases that describe the video. Stay under the 500-character limit and move on to the things that matter more: a clear title, an honest thumbnail and a video that delivers what it promises. Writing Clearer YouTube Titles

A title has two jobs: tell the viewer what they will get, and make them curious enough to click. Beginners often chase the second job and forget the first, which leads to vague titles that attract the wrong audience.

Start with the plain version: "How to Bake Sourdough at Home". Then ask what would make it more specific or more useful: "How to Bake Sourdough at Home (No Special Equipment)". Put the key phrase first, because titles are often cut off on phones and in search results. Many creators keep titles near 60 characters, though YouTube allows up to 100.

Avoid promises the video can't keep. Viewers who feel misled leave quickly, and that hurts you more than a modest title ever would. Try the title generator for editable starting points.

What to Put in a YouTube Description

Only the first lines of a description show before "Show more", so lead with a sentence that says what the video is about and why someone should watch it.

After that, add the useful extras: a short list of what's covered, chapter timestamps for longer videos, links to anything you mention, and a brief note about your channel. Chapters require the first timestamp to be 00:00 and at least three timestamps in order.

Write naturally. Repeating the same keyword many times reads badly and doesn't help. If you'd like a head start, the description generator arranges your own details into a clean layout.
